Entropy, Prediction, and the Physics of Musical Experience
What is the relationship between entropy, the principle of least effort, and entrainment in shaping musical structure and perception?
The second law of thermodynamics dictates that entropy increases in isolated systems, yet biological organisms are paradigmatic examples of open systems that locally decrease entropy — maintaining complex, far-from-equilibrium order at the expense of their surroundings. Music, as a product of biological agents, inherits this tension: it is a highly organized, low-entropy signal embedded in a universe trending toward disorder.
At the same time, information-theoretic entropy — the degree of unpredictability in a sequence — plays a central role in how music is experienced. The brain operates as a predictive engine, continuously generating expectations about incoming sensory input and minimizing prediction error (the free energy principle). The principle of least effort suggests that cognitive systems prefer minimal-surprise trajectories. Yet compelling music systematically violates this principle: composers and performers deliberately introduce surprise, deferred resolution, and violated expectations to generate tension, emotional arousal, and aesthetic reward.
This creates a fundamental question: how does music navigate the space between predictability and surprise — between low and high information-theoretic entropy — to produce its characteristic emotional arc of tension and release? And how does rhythmic entrainment — the synchronization of neural oscillations with periodic temporal structure — interact with harmonic and melodic expectation to shape this experience?
Key sub-questions include:

How do different musical traditions and genres occupy different regions of the predictability-surprise spectrum, and what does this reveal about universal vs. culturally specific aspects of musical cognition?
Can the interplay between thermodynamic entropy (the energetic cost of producing and perceiving organized sound) and information-theoretic entropy (the statistical structure of musical sequences) be formalized in a unified framework?
What is the role of entrainment in establishing a temporal scaffold of expectation against which melodic and harmonic surprises are perceived?
How does the deliberate violation of least-effort predictions relate to dopaminergic reward circuits and the phenomenology of musical chills, groove, and emotional catharsis?
